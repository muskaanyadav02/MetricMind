\# MetricMind Metric Dictionary



\## 1. Purpose



This document defines the governed business metrics used by MetricMind.



These definitions provide a single source of truth for analytics, dashboards,

the Cube semantic layer, and the AI agent.



All metrics must follow these definitions to ensure consistent results across

the system.



\---



\# 2. Metric Summary



| Metric | Definition | Aggregation |

|---|---|---|

| Revenue | Total sales generated | SUM(SALES) |

| Profit | Total profit generated | SUM(PROFIT) |

| Profit Margin | Profit as a percentage of revenue | SUM(PROFIT) / SUM(SALES) × 100 |

| Orders | Number of unique orders | COUNT(DISTINCT ORDER\_ID) |

| Customers | Number of unique customers | COUNT(DISTINCT CUSTOMER\_ID) |

| Quantity Sold | Total quantity of products sold | SUM(QUANTITY) |

| Shipping Cost | Total shipping cost | SUM(SHIPPING\_COST) |

| Average Order Value | Average revenue per unique order | SUM(SALES) / COUNT(DISTINCT ORDER\_ID) |



\---



\# 3. Revenue



\## Business Definition



Revenue represents the total sales value generated from all transaction rows

within the selected filter context.



\## Formula



```text

Revenue = SUM(SALES)





Example



If three transaction rows have:



Sales = 100

Sales = 200

Sales = 300



Then:



Revenue = 100 + 200 + 300

&#x20;       = 600

Important Rule



Revenue must be calculated using SUM(SALES).



4\. Profit

Business Definition



Profit represents the total profit generated by the transactions in the

selected filter context.



Formula

Profit = SUM(PROFIT)

Example

Profit = 50 + (-20) + 100

&#x20;      = 130



Profit can be negative for individual transactions or groups of transactions.



5\. Profit Margin

Business Definition



Profit Margin represents profit as a percentage of total revenue.



Formula

Profit Margin =

&#x20;   SUM(PROFIT) / SUM(SALES) × 100

Example



If:



Revenue = 1,000

Profit  = 100



Then:



Profit Margin = 100 / 1,000 × 100

&#x20;             = 10%

Important Rule



Profit Margin must be calculated from aggregated profit and aggregated sales.



Do not calculate the official metric by averaging transaction-level

profit-margin percentages.



Zero Revenue Handling



If total revenue is zero, Profit Margin should return NULL rather than

performing a division by zero.



6\. Orders

Business Definition



Orders represents the number of unique orders in the selected filter context.



Formula

Orders = COUNT(DISTINCT ORDER\_ID)

Important Rule



The fact table is at transaction/order-line grain.



Therefore:



COUNT(\*) ≠ Number of Orders



One order can contain multiple transaction rows.



Example

Row 1 → ORDER-100

Row 2 → ORDER-100

Row 3 → ORDER-101



There are:



3 transaction rows

2 unique orders



Therefore:



Orders = 2

7\. Customers

Business Definition



Customers represents the number of unique customers associated with the

selected transactions.



Formula

Customers = COUNT(DISTINCT CUSTOMER\_ID)

Example

Customer A

Customer A

Customer B

Customer C



Therefore:



Customers = 3

8\. Quantity Sold

Business Definition



Quantity Sold represents the total number of product units sold.



Formula

Quantity Sold = SUM(QUANTITY)

Example

Quantity = 2

Quantity = 5

Quantity = 3



Therefore:



Quantity Sold = 10

9\. Shipping Cost

Business Definition



Shipping Cost represents the total shipping cost associated with the

transactions in the selected filter context.



Formula

Shipping Cost = SUM(SHIPPING\_COST)

Example

Shipping Cost = 10 + 20 + 15

&#x20;             = 45

10\. Average Order Value

Business Definition



Average Order Value (AOV) represents the average revenue generated per

unique order.



Formula

Average Order Value =

&#x20;   SUM(SALES) / COUNT(DISTINCT ORDER\_ID)

Example



If:



Revenue = 10,000

Orders  = 100



Then:



AOV = 10,000 / 100

&#x20;   = 100

Important Rule



AOV must use the number of unique orders.



It must not divide revenue by the number of transaction rows.



11\. Metric Filter Behavior



All metrics should respect the active analytical filter context.



For example, if a user asks:



What was the revenue in Europe?



the Revenue metric should calculate:



SUM(SALES)



only for the selected European market.



Similarly:



What was the profit margin for 2014?



should calculate:



SUM(PROFIT) / SUM(SALES) × 100



only for transactions belonging to 2014.



12\. Geographic Filtering



The dataset contains multiple geographic attributes:



CITY

STATE

COUNTRY

REGION

MARKET

MARKET2



Business questions should use the appropriate geographic attribute.



For example:



Country → COUNTRY

Region → REGION

Market → MARKET

City → CITY

State → STATE

Europe



For MetricMind's dataset, Europe is represented by:



MARKET = 'EU'



Therefore, European market analysis should use the MARKET field rather than

assuming that REGION = 'Europe'.



13\. Time Filtering



Time-based analysis should use the order date.



Primary date field:



ORDER\_DATE



The date dimension provides:



DAY

MONTH

MONTH\_NAME

QUARTER

YEAR

WEEK\_NUMBER



Examples of supported analytical questions:



Revenue by year

Profit by month

Orders by quarter

Profit margin by year

Revenue growth by month

the calculations.
